Idea → Outline

For stuck fic writers

Got a scene idea that won't turn into a scene?

You know what's supposed to happen. You just can't get it to hang together. This walks you from a loose idea to a structure you can actually write from — in your own words, not mine.

What this is

This isn't a blank-page generator, and it won't invent your plot for you. It's a cheat sheet — a set of questions that get you to nail down what you already half-know (what your characters want, what's standing in their way, where things turn), then hands it back to you as a clean outline. If you've got the raw material but not the shape, this is for you.

Who made this, and why

I'm an Outer Banks fic writer myself, and I've hit this exact wall more times than I can count. I built this because I wanted it to exist, not because I'm trying to sell you anything. It's free, and it always will be.

How it works

Answer a short sequence of questions about your scene. The tool takes your own words and organizes them into a labeled outline — Setup, Tension, Turn, Resolution — plus a check at the end to see if it actually delivers on what you wanted the scene to do. Takes about 5–10 minutes.

I also track anonymous usage (like how many outlines get created) to see if this is actually helping — never what you type. Full details below.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere.

Behind the scenes: how it's built and what I track
How this was built: I designed the questions and outline logic myself, using AI as a drafting and coding assistant along the way — the same way you might use a search engine or a writing craft book. Nothing here uses AI to generate your outline. Your answers are matched into a fixed template through plain code — think mail-merge, not AI.

Your data: This tool runs entirely in your browser. What you type is never sent anywhere, saved, or logged — not by me, not by anyone. Close the tab and it's gone.

Measurement: I track anonymous, aggregate usage only (like how many outlines get generated) — never the content of what you type. The only exception is the optional feedback box at the very end, if you choose to use it — that's sent to me so I can improve the tool.
